Here are a few thoughts I had whilst developing this:

1. WHY THE HECK AM I DOING THIS!
*line_break
[i]I did this, so you don't have to. It is mind-numbingly tedious even using cut+paste and find+replace shortcuts in VScode with collapsible tabs. The endless scrolling, and fixing the indenting — there is a reason I prefer programming languages with closures.[/i]

2. SURELY THERE IS AN EASIER WAY?
*line_break
[i]Unfortunately if you want multiple choices on a page… No, not in (unmodded) ChoiceScript. It is far easier to just have multiple pages in a sequence, a lot more straightforward to write and maintain. So don't go and rush out to start implementing this unless you actually have a use case for it![/i]

3. SO WHY? WHAT IS THE BENEFIT?
*line_break
[i]There is a huge difference between, a player seeing information and choices sequentially, than there is the player seeing them in parallel. In sequential systems you don't know what will be asked next, and so might not know which to choose or the consequences of the choice. Often this is intentional from a writer's point of view, but for some things, like picking an ensemble for a party, it seems more fitting to allow the player to consider multiple choices at the same time and imagine how the interaction of their choices will work out.[/i]

4. IS THIS JUST FOR CHARACTER CREATION THEN?
[i]Whilst character creation systems may stand to benefit through reducing the number of pages required to set up, there are many other situations where making multiple choices simultaneously could greatly enhance the player's experience, such as picking inventory items. The difficulty is of course for the author to parse all the different options in a meaningful way. The largest available here is a 6-multichoice set which has 4^6 (= 4096) unique outcomes. But this is only a selection of 6 things from six sets of 4.[/i]
